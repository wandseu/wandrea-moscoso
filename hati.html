Context

Young Filipino professionals eat out, travel and share household costs in groups. Someone always fronts the bill — then spends the week screenshotting receipts, doing the division, and sending "hey, sa Mesa Grill pala" reminders.

Split-tracking apps exist, but they sit apart from the payment. You pay in one app, log the expense in another, and settle in a third. Every hand-off is a chance for the split to never happen.

The design question

What if splitting wasn't a separate task — just the next step after paying?

Design decisions

01

The split lives inside the receipt

The success screen is where the payer is most motivated to split — the amount is fresh, the friends are still at the table. So "Split this bill?" arrives as a bottom sheet over the confirmation, with the payment already known: merchant, total, time.

"Not now" sits beside "Split" at equal size. Splitting is offered, never pushed.

02

Equal by default, flexible when needed

Most barkada splits are equal, so that's the default: the group is preselected, everyone gets a ₱900 chip, and the button reads "Send requests." Exact, percent and shares are one tap away in a segmented control — there when the lechon was really Paolo's order.

03

Money without blame

Friendships outlast any bill. The copy says "Waiting on Migs," not "Migs hasn't paid," and the action is "Send a friendly nudge." Red is reserved for real errors — never for a friend's balance.

Direction always travels as a set: teal + ↙ + "Owes you" versus coral + ↗ + "You owe". Hue, icon and words together, so it reads without relying on colour alone.

04

One focal point per screen

Each screen had to become a beat in a 15-second animation, so each carries one number or one action — the QR frame, ₱3,600, the checkmark, four avatars, ₱2,700, the wordmark. If a viewer only catches half a second, they still get the point.

05

Playful shape, calm money

I built a small design system for it: ink outlines and hard offset shadows for warmth, teal for trust, mango for highlights. The playfulness is dialled down wherever money appears — amounts are big, tabular and plain.

Visual language

Friendly enough for a barkada, steady enough for money.

Colour

Teal for trust and every primary action, deep teal where white text has to pass contrast. Mango highlights, coral for "you owe", warm sand neutrals.

Type
₱3,600

Plus Jakarta Sans throughout. Amounts get their own styles with tabular numerals and a reduced ₱, so the number is always the loudest thing on screen.

Cards & motion

Ink outlines with hard offset shadows; presses sink into the shadow. Motion is fast — 150ms presses, 400ms sheets — with overshoot saved for the success check.

Reflection

Designing for motion made every screen earn its place.

Knowing each screen would get roughly two seconds on camera forced ruthless editing — one idea per screen, nothing decorative that didn't move the story forward. That constraint made the static design better too.

What I'd explore next

  • The friend's side — receiving a request and settling from wallet, QR or an attached receipt
  • Uneven splits from an itemised receipt, without turning the bill into a spreadsheet
  • Usability testing with real barkadas on how nudges feel from the receiving end
